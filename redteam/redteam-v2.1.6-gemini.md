### Step-by-Step Security Analysis & Red-Team Assessment

#### Step 1: Evaluating the Capability Model Thesis & Label Algebra (§1–§5)

* **Premise Check:** *Is "Powerful extensions do not require powerful extension code" held up by the source/sink/command/actuator closed algebra?*
* **Analysis:**
1. **Closure Coverage:** The enum vocabulary in §5 is now closed. Promoting `probe`, `download_urls`, `user_text`, `browser_traffic`, `native_process`, `enterprise_policy`, `publisher_update`, and `session_state` to explicit enum members closes the implicit-namespace escape hatch.
2. **Readable Egress as Command-Source:** Treating any readable egress context ($network.egress\_public \times \text{actuator}$) as a `remote_server` command-source accurately catches direct C2 scenarios (e.g., polling an endpoint and calling `tabs.navigate`) without needing an intermediate policy-mutation step like `dynamic_rules`.
3. **Publisher vs. Package Principal:** Binding composition to the publisher principal (store-bound organizational identity) stops split-package collusion (e.g., Extension A with source $X$, Extension B with sink $Y$).


* **Verdict on Model Thesis:** Sound. The two-axis closure ($Source \times Sink$ and $Command\text{-}Source \times Actuator$) correctly derives joint authority without relying on naive per-capability permission matching.

#### Step 2: Testing Memory & URL Processing Safety Invariants (§6)

* **Premise Check:** *Does deletion-only byte splicing for `filtering.rewrite_url` remain non-exfiltrating across parameter boundaries?*
* **Raw-First Tokenization Math:**
* Given a raw URL query string: `?a=%26fbclid=secret&b=1`
* Raw tokenization on `&` splits this into:
* Segment 1: `a=%26fbclid=secret`
* Segment 2: `b=1`


* Single-pass decoding during rule-matching on Segment 1 produces key `a` with value `%26fbclid=secret`.
* If a filter rule targets `$removeparam=fbclid`, Segment 1 does **not** match `fbclid` because key `a` is not `fbclid`.


* **Adversarial Reserialization/Evasion Checks:**
* Matching on a normalized scratch copy while **splicing directly from the untouched original byte ranges** prevents character set/normalization laundering (e.g., percent-encoding changes, key-reordering).
* Excluding `;` as a raw separator prevents parameter-injection or truncation attacks on standard WHATWG-compliant endpoints.


* **Verdict on URL Processing:** Structural isolation between matching (normalized) and emission (raw byte range cut) holds the $source: none, sink: none$ invariant intact.

---

#### Step 3: Assessing Statistics, Oracles, and Information Leaks (§7–§8)

* **Premise Check:** *Can single-rule timing/count attacks defeat `stats.read` or scriptlet injections?*
* **Global Privacy Budget Pool:**
* Under §7, `stats.read` returns one dimensionless global counter.
* *Attack Vector:* Launching 100 extensions each holding 1 rule to probe 100 distinct sites in parallel.
* *Mitigation Verification:* The model enforces a **global per-profile privacy budget pool** rather than a per-extension budget. Shared noise drawdown exhausts the probe capacity across all co-installed extensions uniformly, breaking the parallelized oracle attack.


* **Scriptlet Admission Closure (§8):**
* Restricting write values strictly to an allowlist of defusing constants (`false`, `null`, `noopFunc`, etc.) prevents DOM gadget-mediated leaks (e.g., setting `window.config.telemetryUrl = evil`).
* Enforcing *Control-Dependence Non-Interference* ensures that page-derived predicates (`if (page_secret) ...`) cannot conditionally alter extension-observable state.


* **Verdict on Oracles:** Sound, provided the $O(weeks)$ leakage parameter in $O5$ is rigorously calculated at runtime against the global pool.

---

#### Step 4: Assessing Architectural & Boundary Controls (§9–§17)

* **Baleen Artifact Integrity (§14):**
* *Threat:* Hostile or compromised sandboxed compiler producing a malicious mmap-able binary payload.
* *Controls:*
1. Acyclic graph (DAG) or execution step-cap checks prevent match-loop hang Denial-of-Service attacks.
2. Envelope intersection: The executor (Sonar, renderer) intersects the artifact output against a trusted `(extension_id, capability, granted_scope)` envelope generated by the broker. Even if the compiler injects an entry for `bank.example`, the consumer drops it during intersection.
3. Memory sealing: Writable `memfd` descriptors are held exclusively by a privileged assembler; the compiler only sees pipe endpoints, avoiding race conditions prior to `F_SEAL_WRITE`.




* **Sonar Network Isolation (§15):**
* Byte-level validation of header values against RFC 9110 (blocking `\r`, `\n`, `\0`) prevents response-splitting/header-injection bypasses.
* Binding socket connections directly to resolved `SocketAddr` instances prevents DNS-rebinding TOCTOU between policy evaluation and TCP connection.


* **Verdict on Boundaries:** Solid. Privilege separation between compiler, broker, and consumer enforces defense-in-depth even under compiler compromise.

### Final Red-Team Verdict

The **Gosub Extension Capability Model (v2.1.6)** represents an exceptionally robust, structurally sound defense architecture. By decoupling execution authority from raw permissions, enforcing strict byte-level invariants during network/URL mutations, isolating mmap artifacts via separate envelope intersections, and closing the composition algebra over publisher principals and command-source axes, the specification successfully prevents the covert exfiltration and privilege-escalation vectors typical of legacy extension systems (MV2/MV3). The remaining operational dependencies ($O1$–$O8$) represent implementation/verification steps rather than structural flaws in the model logic itself.
